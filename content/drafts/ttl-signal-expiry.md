What is signal TTL in spot market intelligence? Time-to-live defines how long a spot recommendation remains valid before expiring. At RefineX, every signal carries an expiry timestamp, and we suppress signals built from stale data rather than deliver outdated advice that could trigger poor placement decisions.

A spot recommendation built on 15-minute-old pricing data is not advice. It is historical trivia. This fundamental principle drives our TTL implementation across the signal pipeline.

## How RefineX Implements Signal Expiry

Every signal generated by RefineX includes three timestamp fields in the database schema. The `created_at` field marks when the signal was generated. The `ttl` field specifies the validity window in seconds. The `expires_at` field calculates the absolute expiration moment using `created_at + ttl`.

Our expiration worker runs every minute to identify and suppress expired signals. The process queries all active signals where `expires_at` has passed, marks them as inactive by setting `is_active = False`, and logs the expiration count for monitoring purposes. We process expiration as a scheduled job rather than on-demand to ensure consistent cleanup regardless of API request patterns.

The worker implementation prioritizes reliability over efficiency. Each expiration cycle opens a fresh database session, processes all expired signals in a single transaction, and closes cleanly. We log success and failure states separately to distinguish between zero expired signals and actual processing errors.

## Why Stale Data Gets Suppressed

Spot pricing changes rapidly across AWS regions and availability zones. A signal recommending c5.large in us-east-1a based on pricing captured at 14:30 may be worthless by 14:45 if the underlying spread has collapsed or reversed. We treat this staleness as a confidence killer, not a minor degradation.

When our public API encounters an expired signal during retrieval, the system derives a suppression reason based on the expiry context. Signals that expired due to age get marked with `ttl_expired`. Signals with confidence scores below our threshold receive `confidence_below_threshold`. Signals built from data sources older than five minutes get flagged as `stale_data`.

This suppression logic appears in our public transparency endpoint, which returns both delivered and suppressed signals without authentication. The endpoint calculates suppression rates in real-time, showing the percentage of signals blocked before delivery. Today's suppression rate sits at 47.7% over the past two hours, meaning we blocked nearly half of generated signals to maintain quality standards.

## The Cost of Conservative TTL Settings

Our current TTL configuration errs toward aggressive expiration. Most spot arbitrage signals expire within 10-15 minutes of generation. Interruption risk signals may carry longer TTL values, but still expire within 30 minutes maximum. This conservative approach increases our suppression rate but reduces the likelihood of customers acting on outdated intelligence.

The tradeoff manifests in signal volume versus signal freshness. Longer TTL windows would increase our delivery rate and reduce the percentage of suppressed signals visible in our [transparency log](https://www.refinex.io/transparency). However, longer windows also increase the risk of delivering recommendations built on pricing spreads that no longer exist.

We optimize for accuracy over volume. A suppressed signal cannot cause a poor placement decision. A delivered signal with stale underlying data can trigger customers to launch instances in suboptimal zones or instance families, leading to unnecessary interruptions or missed savings opportunities.

## How TTL Expiry Affects Signal Scoring

The expiry system operates independently from our deterministic scoring pipeline. Confidence calculations, regime classification, and expected value computations complete before TTL evaluation. A signal may achieve high confidence scores and attractive savings projections, but still face suppression if the underlying data inputs have aged beyond acceptable freshness thresholds.

This separation ensures that TTL expiry functions as a quality gate rather than a scoring input. We do not reduce confidence scores for signals approaching expiry. Instead, we suppress expired signals entirely, treating staleness as a binary pass-fail criterion rather than a gradual degradation factor.

The expiry worker processes signals that have already passed through confidence filtering and regime analysis. High-quality signals with strong expected value projections still get suppressed if their data inputs have expired. This approach prevents the delivery of technically sound recommendations built on outdated market conditions.

## Monitoring Expiry Patterns

We track expiry rates as a leading indicator of market volatility and data pipeline health. During periods of rapid AWS spot repricing, TTL expiry rates spike as pricing spreads shift faster than our collection and processing cycles can accommodate. Normal expiry rates hover between 40-50% of generated signals. Rates above 60% typically indicate increased market volatility or data source latency issues.

The expiry scheduler logs successful cleanup operations with the count of expired signals. These metrics feed into our operational dashboards and help identify patterns in signal lifetime versus market conditions. Extended periods of high expiry rates may prompt TTL adjustment or data source optimization to reduce the lag between market changes and signal generation.
